NCERT Class 12 Chapter 11

Organisms and Populations — High-Yield Revision Summary

Core ecological concepts, formulas, population growth models, life history strategies, and interspecific interaction matrices.

1. History & Ecological Levels of Organisation

• Father of Ecology in India: Prof. Ramdeo Misra (1908–1998). Established the first postgraduate course in ecology at Banaras Hindu University (BHU), Varanasi. Formulated the National Committee for Environmental Planning and Coordination (1972) leading to the Ministry of Environment and Forests (1984).

• Four Levels of Biological Organisation in Ecology: Organisms → Populations → Communities → Biomes.

2. Population Attributes & Density Dynamics

Population Rates & Density

• Birth rate (Natality) & Death rate (Mortality): Expressed as per capita change. E.g., 8 dead out of 80 fruit flies in a week = 8/80 = 0.1 individuals per fruitfly per week.

• Density Equation: $N_{t+1} = N_t + [(B + I) - (D + E)]$

• Increasing density factors: Natality (B) and Immigration (I).

• Decreasing density factors: Mortality (D) and Emigration (E).

Age Pyramids for Human Population

• Expanding (Growing): Triangular pyramid with large pre-reproductive base.

• Stable: Bell-shaped pyramid with pre-reproductive and reproductive age groups nearly equal.

• Declining: Urn-shaped pyramid with constricted pre-reproductive base.

3. Population Growth Curves & Mathematical Models

Exponential Growth (J-shaped Curve)

• Occurs when resources (food and space) are unlimited.

• Differential equation: $\frac{dN}{dt} = (b - d)N = rN$

• Integral form: $N_t = N_0 e^{rt}$

• r is the intrinsic rate of natural increase. Values: Norway rat = 0.015, flour beetle = 0.12, human in India (1981) = 0.0205.

Verhulst-Pearl Logistic Growth (Sigmoid Curve)

• Realistic model because environmental resources are finite and limiting.

• Differential equation: $\frac{dN}{dt} = rN\left(\frac{K - N}{K}\right)$

• K = Carrying capacity (maximum population habitat can support).

• Phases: Lag phase → Acceleration → Deceleration → Asymptote (at $N = K$).

4. Summary of Interspecific Interactions (NCERT Table 11.1)

Mutualism + + Lichens (fungus + alga), Mycorrhiza (Glomus + roots), Fig tree & blastophaga wasp, Mediterranean orchid Ophrys & male bee (sexual deceit / pseudocopulation).
Competition - - Flamingoes & resident fish competing for zooplankton in South American lakes; Abingdon tortoise in Galapagos extinct after goats introduced; Barnacle Balanus excluding Chthamalus; MacArthur's 5 warbler species coexisting via resource partitioning.
Predation + - Tiger & deer; sparrow eating seed; Prickly pear cactus in Australia controlled by cactophagous moth; Starfish Pisaster maintaining intertidal diversity; Calotropis producing poisonous cardiac glycosides against herbivores; Monarch butterfly distasteful due to weed toxin.
Parasitism + - Human liver fluke (two intermediate hosts: snail and fish); Malarial parasite & mosquito vector; Ectoparasites: lice on humans, ticks on dogs, marine copepods on fish, Cuscuta on hedge plants; Brood parasitism: Cuckoo (koel) laying eggs in crow's nest.
Commensalism + 0 Orchid growing as an epiphyte on a mango branch; Barnacles on back of whale; Cattle egret foraging near grazing cattle; Sea anemone with stinging tentacles hosting clown fish.
Amensalism - 0 Penicillium secreting penicillin that inhibits bacterial growth without benefit/harm to itself.
